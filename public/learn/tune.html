RAG & Enterprise AI

Prompting vs RAG vs fine-tuning for product managers

Picking the wrong technique burns months and budget. Start with prompting, add RAG when the model needs your knowledge, and fine-tune only to change behaviour or style at scale.

There are three main levers to improve an LLM feature. Prompting changes the instructions and examples, which is the fastest and cheapest option. RAG gives the model the right knowledge at query time by retrieving your documents. Fine-tuning further trains a model on examples so it reliably behaves a certain way. They solve different problems and are often combined.

A useful rule: if the model doesn't know something (your policies, product data, recent facts), use RAG. If it knows enough but doesn't behave the way you need (format, tone, a narrow task done consistently), improve the prompt first, then consider fine-tuning. Fine-tuning is a poor way to add facts that change, because you'd have to retrain every time they do.

Escalate in order, prompting then RAG then fine-tuning, and let your evaluation set decide each step. Fine-tuning brings extra costs: preparing high-quality training examples, training runs, hosting, and re-tuning when better base models arrive. It can pay off at high volume, for example when a smaller tuned model replaces a larger one to cut cost and latency. Ask your engineers: what does the eval show for each option, and what will it cost to maintain?
