Prompt engineering for product managers

Prompts are your cheapest prototype. Good prompts give role, context, task, constraints and output format. Few-shot examples steer style; structured (JSON) output makes results usable by software.

A prompt is the spec you give the model. Strong prompts include a role (who the model is acting as), context (background and data), the task, constraints (tone, length, what not to do) and the output format. Treat it like a product brief: a vague brief gets a vague result.

A few techniques do most of the work. Few-shot examples show the model the style you want and how to handle edge cases. Structured output (for example JSON with a defined schema) makes results usable by software. Breaking complex work into steps (prompt chaining), asking the model to reason before answering, and telling it what to do when it's unsure all make results more reliable.

For PMs, prompts are the cheapest prototype there is: you can test an idea on 20 real examples in an afternoon before any engineering starts. In production, prompts are product assets - version them and re-run your test set whenever they change, because one sentence can shift behaviour. And know the limits: prompting can't fix missing data, or a model that is too slow or expensive for the job.
