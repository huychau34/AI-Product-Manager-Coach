AI Product Strategy

AI strategy, economics & metrics for product managers

Turns features into a defensible roadmap. Decide build vs buy, where your proprietary data creates advantage, and unit economics (cost per task vs. price/value). Pair adoption metrics with quality and cost metrics.

Models are becoming commodities: your competitors can use the same models you do, so the model alone is rarely a moat. Durable advantage comes from proprietary data and feedback loops, deep workflow integration, distribution, trust, and domain expertise.

On build vs buy: buy (use a vendor or API) when AI isn't your differentiator and speed matters; build or customise where it's core to your value, where data is sensitive, or where unit economics at scale demand it. Either way, keep your architecture model-agnostic so you can switch vendors as price and quality change.

AI changes economics: every use has a cost, which affects margins and pricing (per seat, per usage, or per outcome). Track adoption (active use, retention) together with quality (acceptance, accuracy) and cost (cost per task, gross margin). Retention is the real test - many AI features see a spike of curiosity and then drop off.
