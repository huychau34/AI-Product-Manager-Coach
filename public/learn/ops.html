Evaluation, Safety & Operations

Launching & monitoring AI features for product managers

Quality in the demo is not quality in production. Roll out gradually, log and trace AI interactions, monitor quality, cost and latency, and have a plan for incidents.

AI features keep changing after launch: users ask things you never tested, data changes, and model providers release new versions. So launch like an experiment. Use a staged rollout (internal users, then a small percentage, then everyone), keep a feature flag or kill switch, and where possible compare against the old experience with an A/B test.

You can't improve what you can't see. Log and trace AI interactions (inputs, retrieved context, outputs and user reactions) with privacy in mind. Monitor quality signals such as acceptance, edits, thumbs and escalations, plus latency and cost per task, and set alerts when they move. Pin model versions, and re-run your eval set before any prompt, model or data change goes live.

Plan for incidents before they happen. Decide what counts as an AI incident (a harmful answer, a data leak, a quality collapse), who is on call, how to switch the feature off or fall back, and how you'll tell users. After each incident, add the failing cases to your eval set so the same failure can't return unnoticed. This operational discipline is often called LLMOps.
