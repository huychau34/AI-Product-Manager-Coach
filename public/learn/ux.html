AI Product Design

Designing AI UX & trust for product managers

Users decide in seconds whether to trust an AI feature. Good AI UX sets expectations, shows why the AI said something, makes errors easy to spot and fix, and learns from feedback.

AI features are probabilistic, so the UX has to do work that traditional software never needed. Users need to understand what the AI can and can't do, when to rely on it, and how to check it. Onboarding, empty states and examples set those expectations before the first interaction. Overpromising at this stage is the fastest way to lose trust later.

The goal is calibrated trust: users rely on the AI when it's right and catch it when it's wrong. Tools that help: showing sources or the evidence behind an answer, signalling uncertainty, letting users edit before anything is applied, and keeping a clear undo. Match the level of control to the stakes: a draft reply can auto-fill, but a payment or a deletion needs explicit confirmation.

Design for failure and feedback from day one. When the AI can't help, say so plainly and offer a next step or a manual path instead of a vague error. Make feedback effortless (thumbs, quick reasons, or simply tracking edits), because that data drives error analysis, evals and improvements. Two PM questions to always ask: what does the user see when the model is wrong, and how will we find out?

Practise it

Questions like these are in the lesson, with instant feedback:

  • Users of a product team over-trust an AI suggestion that is sometimes wrong. Best design fix?
  • Which help users calibrate their trust in AI output? (select all)
  • Why collect thumbs up/down or track edits on AI output?
