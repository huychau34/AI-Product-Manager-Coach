RAG & Enterprise AI

Embeddings & RAG for product managers

How AI uses your company's private knowledge. Embeddings turn text into vectors so similar meaning is close. RAG retrieves relevant documents first, then has the LLM answer from them: no retraining, and answers can cite sources.

Embeddings turn text into lists of numbers (vectors) where similar meanings sit close together. That's how a search for "refund policy" can find a document about "returns and money back" without matching keywords. A vector database stores these vectors and finds the closest ones quickly.

RAG (retrieval-augmented generation) works in two steps. Beforehand, documents are split into chunks, embedded and indexed. When a user asks something, the system retrieves the most relevant chunks, puts them into the prompt, and the LLM answers from them - and can cite them. No retraining is needed; you update knowledge by re-indexing. Fine-tuning is better for changing behaviour or style, not for facts that change often.

Answer quality depends mostly on retrieval: poor chunking, stale or conflicting documents, or missing permissions lead to wrong answers. PM decisions include which sources are in scope and who owns them, how fresh they must be, access control (users should only retrieve what they're allowed to see), showing citations, and what to say when nothing relevant is found. Measure retrieval quality separately from answer quality.
