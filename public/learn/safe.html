Responsible AI, safety & compliance for product managers

Safety, privacy and regulation can block a launch late, or protect it. Identify harms early (bias, privacy, misuse), add guardrails and red-teaming, and know which rules, such as the EU AI Act, apply.

Responsible AI is about anticipating harm before users experience it. The main risk areas for PMs are fairness (does it work equally well for different groups?), privacy and security (can it leak personal or confidential data?), misuse (can someone trick it, for example with prompt injection?) and reliability in high-stakes decisions. Treat these like any other product risk: identify, prioritise, mitigate and monitor.

Guardrails turn principles into product behaviour: input and output filters, strict access control for retrieved data, limits on which actions an AI can take, and human review for consequential decisions. Red-teaming, deliberately trying to break the system with adversarial prompts and edge cases, reveals failures that normal testing misses. Log incidents and feed them back into your evals.

Regulation is now part of the roadmap. The EU AI Act is risk-based: some uses are banned, high-risk uses (such as hiring or credit decisions) carry strict requirements for data, documentation and human oversight, and many systems have transparency duties, such as telling users they're talking to an AI. Frameworks like NIST's AI Risk Management Framework offer a practical structure. Involve legal and privacy colleagues early; it's much cheaper than a late redesign.
