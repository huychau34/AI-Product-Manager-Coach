AI Product Strategy

Pricing & monetising AI features for product managers

Every AI request costs money, so pricing must cover it and capture value. Understand AI unit economics, choose between seat, usage and outcome pricing, and protect margins as usage grows.

Unlike traditional software, AI features have a real marginal cost: every request uses compute. Start with unit economics, comparing the cost per task (tokens, retrieval, infrastructure, human review) with the value created per task. That tells you whether a feature can be bundled for free, needs usage limits, or should be priced separately.

There are three main pricing models, often combined. Seat-based pricing (sometimes with a paid AI add-on) is simple for buyers but risky when usage varies a lot. Usage-based pricing (credits, per task) aligns price with cost but makes spend less predictable. Outcome-based pricing (per resolved ticket, per qualified lead) aligns price with customer value, but needs outcomes that are measurable and clearly caused by your product.

Pricing is a product decision, not just a finance one. Talk to customers about the value they get and what they'd pay for, set usage limits or fair-use tiers to prevent runaway costs, and track gross margin per feature as usage grows. Protect margins with engineering levers too: smaller models where quality allows, caching, shorter prompts and batching.
